## Overview

Business Architecture is a representation of holistic, multi-dimensional business views of: capabilities, end-to-end value delivery, information, and organizational structure; and the relationships among these business views and strategies, products, policies, initiatives, and stakeholders. Business Architecture should be considered as a subset of an overall Enterprise Architecture.

In the business sector, business architecture is a discipline that "represents holistic, multidimensional business views of: capabilities, end‐to‐end value delivery, information, and organizational structure; and the relationships among these business views and strategies, products, policies, initiatives, and stakeholders." In application, business architecture provides a bridge between an enterprise business model and enterprise strategy on one side, and
the business functionality of the enterprise on the other side. It often enables the **Strategy to Execution** methodology. *People who develop and maintain business architecture are known as **business architects**.*

Business Architecture covers the following core aspects of a business: **capabilities, information, organization, and value streams** (as defined in the BIZBOK® Guide). According to the BIZBOK® Guide, the core of the business architecture is comprised of business capability, organization, information, and value streams. These four business domains enable representation of complex business perspectives for large-scale organizations on an enterprise scale.

- Capability
	• Understanding what a business does is just as important as understanding how something is being done.
	• The capability map in its entirety delivers a concise, non-redundant, business-centric view of the business at its most basic level, and it encompasses a complete picture of what that business does.
	• Capabilities represent the basic building blocks of a business.
- Value
	• Value Map shows how an organization creates the value being exchanges between itself and its stakeholders.
	• Value Proposition is an innovation, service, or feature intended to make a company, product, or service attractive to customers or related stakeholders. In a nutshell:
	• Explains how your product solves customers’ problems or improves their situation
	• Delivers specific benefits (quantified value)
	• Tells why customer should buy from you and not from the competition.
- Information
	• Extract information concepts from capabilities; define information concepts
	• Identify types/use of information concepts
	• Validate the information map
- Organization
	• Organization Map provides visibility on a business
	• Describes business units and communication approach

## Business Architecture Example

Steps to define and actualize business architecture:

![[Pasted image 20250420172823.png]]

A Business Architecture example will illustrate how to define and actualize business architecture for an enterprise. Imagine there is a giant conglomerate with several lines of business spread across the globe. The enterprise has grown both organically and inorganically, resulting in a complicated business and IT landscape. The company has a plethora of patchwork systems, arcane processes, and cumbersome procedures.

**Step 1**

The first step is to receive an understanding of the vision, mission, and purpose of the enterprise. Understanding what the company is and what it stands for is an essential first step in modeling the business's essence. Via a detailed examination of public and strategy documents and discussions with senior executives target operating model (a blueprint of a firm's business vision that aligns operating capacities and strategic objectives and provides an overview of the core business capabilities, internal factors, and external drivers, organizational and functional structure, technology, and information resources) can be defined.

**Step 2**

At this stage, a business model canvas (a visual chart with elements describing a firm's or product's value proposition, infrastructure, customers, and finances) should be created to understand the context, customers, drivers, and resources inherent to the success of the firm.

**Step 3**

In this step, the focus is on business capabilities. In this example, a sample business capability map is used as a starting point. With a sample business capability model, the company can absorb, ponder, analyze, and iteratively refine the business capabilities. The team also has documented definition and high-level capability-level profile information, including capability assessments. In this example, the iterative refinement will continue over a period as more teams participate in the business architecture development and when the business capability map is put into use.

**Step 4**

For Value Streams, there is the need to collaborate with the process experts to abstract the specific flows into a high level, end-to-end stakeholder-based activity streams. For example, the group documented several value streams such as prospect to customer, quote to purchase, order to cash, issue to resolution, insight to the decision, etc. 

**Step 5**
Now, there is a need to map the capabilities to value streams to create a view that combined the "What" (capabilities) with the "How" (value streams and processes). To each step/stage of the value flows, the underlying deeper level processes for a more in-depth understanding were associated.

**Step 6**

With the help of Enterprise and Software architects, the inventory of applications, systems, and IT services is taken and mapped to the capabilities and value streams. This footprint analysis can help understand the IT enablement of various capabilities and the coverage, redundancy, and replication. These views—Capabilities to Systems and Value Streams to Systems—are an essential business/technology deliverable that will be used in transformation and optimization decisions.

**Step 7**

Now, with the assistance of the data team, the company-wide data subject areas and data entities can be understood. Then, this information is juxtaposed with the capabilities and value streams to gain an understanding of what data is involved in which capabilities and value streams/processes. Now, other components like locations, structure, roles, channels, stakeholders, business rules, KPIs, product offerings can be identified.

**Step 8**

The key strategic pillars and the initiatives are mapped to capabilities, including allocating budget at the capability level. It is now possible to figure out what changes need to be made to capabilities to evolve them to a state that the company wants/requires them to be. This reflects in the capability-based transformation roadmap.
