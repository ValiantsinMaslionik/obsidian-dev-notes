
## Typical Activities

#### Pre-sales

- Request for information (RFI) and Request For Proposal (RFP) calls
- Estimates
- Architecture design
- Migration plans
- Deployment diagrams
- Project roadmaps
- Answering technical sections in RFPs
- On-site presentations for clients

#### Production

- Architecture design and governance
- Discovery, project kick-off
- Architecture/code reviews
- Everything else required for a successful delivery

#### Non-Production ("EPAM Production")

- Interviewing
- Assessment
- Competency Center's activities
- Education (SAS trainings, SA mentoring, tech talks, etc.)

#### Pre-Sales

1. Participate in calls with clients
2. Prepares questions for a customer during RFP processing and provides answers to a customer's questions as a part of RFI
3. Gathers requirements and conduct analysis. A SA must focus only on architecturally significant requirements.
4. A SA can do architecture design and technical design. However, this is done at a high level because of lack of time and requirements.
5. Estimates and drafts a high level resource plan which is necessary and sufficient for this stage
6. Gives on-site presentations together with sales/account managers.
7. Develop project roadmaps.

SA is not a BA (business analyst) - these two should work together at this stage. A BA should go in-depth into the requirements. The most efficient setup for each pre-sale includes a SA, a BA, as well as sales and DM.

## Architecture Design

1. Analyze business drivers and goals
2. Works with stakeholders, managing their expectations and replying properly to their concerns
3. Gathering and prioritizes requirements (ASRs)
4. Defines main quality attributes and selects appropriate tactics to archive them (tactics, styles, patterns, etc.)
5. Describes current solution architecture (as-is)
6. Ensures that the transition is planned and implemented properly
7. Design target architecture and documents it.

## Architecture review

Review is a process where architectural decisions are evaluated as to how they enable to restrict the system to meet its architecturally significant requirements.

1. VALIDATE that the architecture is capable of supporting the current and future business goals and needs.
2. ASSESS the architecture's ability to meet quality attribute requirements
3. DETECT design errors early in the Software Development Life Cycle
4. IDENTIFY potential risks to the project

## Architecture governance

Architecture governance is the practice and orientation by which architectures are managed and controlled at an enterprise-wide level.

#### Control

Implementing a system of controls over the creation and monitoring architectural components and activities to ensure the effective introduction, implementation and evolution of architectures within the organization.

#### Compliance

Implementing a system to ensure compliance with internal and external standards and regulatory obligations.

#### Management

Establishing processes that support effective management of the above processes within agreed parameters.

#### Accountability

Developing practices that ensures accountability (подотчётность) to a clearly identified stakeholder community, both inside and outside the organization.

## Typical activities of a solution architect include

- Architecture Review: Assessing existing system architectures to identify improvements and ensure alignment with business objectives.
- Pre-Sales: Is relevant in client-facing roles where they assist sales teams by designing solutions to meet client requirements and demonstrating technical feasibility.
- Architecture Design: Creating efficient, scalable, and sustainable architectural solutions.
