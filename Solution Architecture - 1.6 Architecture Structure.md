
## View and Viewpoints

A **view** is a representation of a system's elements and their relations. Only some of a system's elements are shown in one view. Also, a view places certain constraints on the types of elements and relations it represents.

- **Structure** - is a set of a system's elements and their organization.
- **Viewpoint** -  is a way of looking at a system
- **View** - is what you see. **A view is a representation of as structure.**

For example, a module structure isa set of a system's modules and their organization. A module view is the representation of that structure, documented according to a template in a chosen notation, and used by some system stakeholders.

View > Structure

> **Architects design structures.** They document views of those structures.

A view is what you see.
A view is the result of applying a viewpoint to a particular system of interest.

A viewpoint is where you are looking from - the vantage point of perspective that determines what you see. A viewpoint is defines conventions, for example, notations, languages and types of models for constructing a certain kind of view.

So, each stakeholder looks at the system from their own viewpoint and sees one more relevant things. The CEO of an organization sees one view, the architect sees other views.
