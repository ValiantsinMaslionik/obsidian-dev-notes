#### Big up-front design

Actually, each system should be designed and documented in a reasonable way. We need to analyze
current needs and provide as much design as is required and enough to start the project or to make it successful.

#### Longer releases

In fact, architecture design approaches are also iterative and incremental, and we will see this during
one of the next modules called Architecture Design. It's important to understand now that creating
design is an incremental process. Every day, something happens, meaning new requirements come,
new business goals or maybe business goals change. And it’s absolutely Okay to update the design
incrementally.

#### Massive documentation

It's perfectly fine to have a small amount of documentation if it’s sufficient for your concrete project. If it’s enough for you to have just confluence pages with photos of your white-boarding, it’s fine. There is no sense in creating 100 pages of architecture documents that are never used after they created.

#### Architects are decision makers
Solution architects’ decisions are based on stakeholder needs. Therefore, a primary task is to identify
and understand the business goal and all architectural decisions including creating design, selecting a technology stack, selecting tactics for achieving quality attributes should be based on these business goals.

#### Decisions are hard to reverse
There is an initiative called “Evolutionary Architecture” and it’s about an effective approach to creating architecture which supports incremental changes. Evolutionary architectures are organized around business capabilities; they are fine-grained, modular and so on.

#### Long-term Design and Incremental Design

While solution architects create their design, they should do it incrementally and iteratively.

#### Low visible value
As stated earlier, in some cases there is no sense in doing big up-front design. We need to concentrate on concrete business goals and high-priority business features instead.
